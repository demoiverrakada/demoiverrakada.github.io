Hi, I'm Umang.

I'm an independent AI safety researcher based in India. Alongside research, I work in software engineering at Amazon.

Most of my work comes back to one question: when a measurement of an AI model looks good, does it actually track the behavior we care about? In practice that means auditing what finetuning really teaches a model, testing whether safety evaluations measure what they claim, and trying to detect models that deliberately underperform (sandbagging).

I like experiments that make it hard to fool yourself. I write down the question and what would count as success before looking at results, and when a control fails, I stop and report it rather than rewriting the claim. Negative results are results.

What I'm working on now

Finetune Trace: can model-diffing methods recover the behavior a finetune installed in a model, rather than just what its training data was about? Stage 1 is complete and Stage 1b is in progress.

Exploratory studies

  • Black-box vs. white-box sandbagging detection
    Three ways to catch a model that deliberately underperforms; the black-box one caught nothing at a 1% false-positive rate.
  • Evaluation-awareness control validation
    Stopped before training, because the supposedly neutral controls were not neutral.
  • Refusal Cartography
    Mapping what drives a model's refusals, to predict attacks it has never tried. Early stage.
  • LLM red-teaming evaluation pipeline
    How often Llama 3.2 3B refusals hold: 48.4% attack success, 23% false refusals.
  • AI alignment and safety benchmarks
    Separating what a model can do, what it will do, and whether controls can stop it.
